MageStone — Complete Rules

MageStone is played by 2 or 4 players (there is no 3-player game) on a 16×16 board with cut corners. Each player commands 1 Mage, 1 Priest and 6 Warriors, arrayed along their home edge as Warrior · Warrior · Warrior · Priest · Mage · Warrior · Warrior · Warrior. The central 8×8 holds the MageStones (4 per player: 8 for two players, 16 for four); the centre 2×2 is the Nexus.

Rounds and activations

Play is not turn-at-a-time — players alternate activations within a shared round.

  1. Every player rolls 5 dice at the start of the round: 1 Mage die, 1 Priest die, 3 Warrior dice (all d6).
  2. Nothing is discarded. All five stay available and visible, but each player may spend only 3 of their 5 per round. Unused dice are simply ignored when the round ends.
  3. Take one activation, then pass. An activation is: choose 1 unused die, activate its matching unit, move it up to the die's value, and immediately resolve that unit's action. Then play passes to the next player.
  4. Same-colour bundle — the one exception to alternating. You may spend 2 or 3 unused dice of the same colour in a single activation: all their units move and resolve together (so Warriors can march in and coordinate an attack as one activation) and play passes only when the whole bundle is finished.
  5. Players keep alternating until nobody has dice left to spend. Then the round ends, everyone rolls again, and the starting player alternates each round.

Movement is an orthogonal route that may bend, up to the die value, through empty squares; units block, stones and gravestones do not. A die only ever activates its matching unit kind, and a unit may take only one die per round. Actions are: attack, collect MageStones, activate MageStones, resurrect, or begin the ritual.

Combat

Warriors and Mages attack an enemy on an orthogonally adjacent square; Priests never attack. One Warrior rolls 1d6; two or three Warriors adjacent to the same target coordinate for 2d6 or 3d6 summed. The Mage rolls its power die — d6 with 0–1 activated MageStones, d12 with 2–3, d20 with 4+.

The defender rolls 1d6 (a defending Mage rolls its own power die). Higher total wins and ties go to the attacker. If the attacker wins, the target is defeated; if the defender wins, one attacker falls — except against a defending Priest, which never kills its attacker and may instead flee up to the value of its defence roll.

Mage sorcery

Activated MageStones can be spent as spells. A spent stone is never destroyed and never deactivated — it leaves the Mage, lands back on the board still Activated, and can be claimed by any Mage, including an opponent's.

  • Bolt (1 Activated stone) — an indefensible ranged attack on any enemy within the mage die's value in orthogonal steps. No defence roll is made by anything, a Mage included; the target is defeated immediately. The spent stone lands on the square that was hit, still Activated.
  • Nova (4 Activated stones) — an indefensible area attack. Every enemy unit in the 8 squares surrounding the Mage is defeated with no defence roll; friendly units are unharmed. The four spent stones are placed on the four diagonal squares around the Mage, all still Activated.

Defeat, gravestones and respawns

  • A fallen Warrior leaves a gravestone — only while the shared bank still holds one, never on the Nexus, and never stacked on another gravestone. The bank is finite and never replenished: it starts at 4 per player (8 in a 2-player game, 16 in a 4-player game), a Warrior's death spends one, and a resurrection removes that gravestone from the game rather than returning it. Once the bank empties, Warrior losses are permanent.
  • A fallen Mage drops its Unactivated stones plus exactly one Activated stone where it fell, keeps the rest, and respawns at its base. The dropped stone stays Activated. A fallen Priest respawns at its base.
  • If an enemy occupies your base, you are under siege: respawns wait until the base is clear. A player whose last units are locked out is eliminated.

MageStones

The Mage collects a stone by ending its move on it, and may activate carried stones only while standing on its own base. Activated stones power the Mage's combat die (d6 with 0–1, d12 with 2–3, d20 with 4–5) and count toward victory; Unactivated stones do neither.

Activation is permanent. Once a MageStone has ever been Activated it can never become Unactivated again — not when it is dropped, not when its Mage is killed, not when it is spent on a spell, and not when an enemy takes it. A Mage that picks up an already-Activated stone counts it immediately and does not need to carry it home. More of the board's stones therefore become instantly useful as a game goes on.

The Priest

A Priest standing on any gravestone may resurrect a Warrior there (up to 6 alive), stepping back one square; that gravestone then leaves the game for good. Normally only one gravestone may be resurrected per turn. A Priest that wins its defence roll may flee up to the value of that roll, and if it lands on a gravestone it may resurrect from it immediately, even outside its own turn. A Priest in the Nexus with all four Nexus squares free of enemies may begin the ritual.

Victory

  1. MageStone — your Mage stands on your own base holding 6 Activated MageStones. Instant win. Picking up a sixth Activated stone away from home does not win on its own — the Mage still has to reach its base.
  2. Ritual — your Priest still holds a clear Nexus when play returns to you a full round after beginning the ritual.
  3. Conquest — every rival is eliminated.
